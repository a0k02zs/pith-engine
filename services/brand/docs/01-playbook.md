Version 1 · 21 Oct 2026 · Owner: content and brand lead (Pith M)

## 1. What this service is for

This service gives each client a clear position and a brand system that people and agents can use.
Its main output is the **client brain**: the brand profile that every other Pith M agent reads
before it writes. If the brand is weak or unclear, all 17 other services produce weak work.

The plan sets automation at about 30%. This is the lowest level of the 18 services, for a reason:

- The decisions here are about identity. The client must own them.
- A logo made only by AI may not get copyright protection in some markets (see `05-market-rules.md`).
- Trademark checks need a person, and in the end a lawyer.

AI does the research, the first drafts, the variants, the checks and the conversion into the client brain.
People make the choices.

## 2. The 3 packages we sell

| Package | Who it is for | What the client gets | Typical length |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A · Brand-to-brain** | Clients with a brand that works, who buy other Pith M services | Audit of current brand assets, voice chart, approved claims, banned words, personas, loaded client brain, brand score calibrated | 2 weeks |
| **B · Brand refresh** | Clients whose brand is out of date or not consistent | Package A, plus new positioning, messaging house, voice refresh, visual refresh (colour, type, imagery; logo tidy-up), guidelines, templates | 5 to 7 weeks |
| **C · Full brand build** | New companies, rebrands, mergers, entry into a new market | Package B, plus naming (optional), new logo and identity, market versions (Arabic, Quebec French), trademark screen, full template library, launch kit | 9 to 12 weeks |

Rules:

1. Every client of any Pith M service gets at least Package A. Without a client brain, no other service starts.
2. Package A is part of onboarding. The head of growth sets the price of each package.
3. Package C names are optional. A name project adds about 3 weeks and needs a lawyer.

## 3. The brand system we build

Each client gets the same 8 blocks. The names stay the same across clients, so agents can find them.

| # | Block | Contents | Loaded into the client brain? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Insight base | Category map, competitor audit, voice of the customer, interview summary | Summary only |
| 2 | Positioning | Target, frame of reference, point of difference, reason to believe (RTB), 1-line statement | Yes |
| 3 | Messaging house | Brand promise, 3 to 4 pillars, proof points per pillar, messages per persona and buying stage | Yes |
| 4 | Personas | 3 to 5 personas, with jobs, pains, gains, objections, channels, per market | Yes |
| 5 | Verbal identity | Voice traits, tone by situation, vocabulary, banned words, grammar and spelling rules per market, taglines, boilerplate | Yes |
| 6 | Claims register | Approved claims with evidence and expiry date; claims that need approval; forbidden claims | Yes |
| 7 | Visual identity | Logo set, colour, type, imagery, iconography, layout grid, motion basics, accessibility rules | Tokens and rules: yes. Files: links |
| 8 | Templates | Social, email, ad, slide, document, video lower-thirds, in Figma and Canva | Links and IDs |

## 4. B2B and B2C differences

| Topic | B2B focus | B2C focus |
|---|---|---|
| Research | Win/loss interviews, sales call notes, analyst views, G2 and review sites | Reviews, social comments, search queries, survey, store or app data |
| Positioning | Category and use case; risk reduction; proof (case studies, numbers) | Emotion and identity; occasion; price and value signal |
| Personas | Buying committee: user, champion, economic buyer, IT/security, procurement | Segments by need, life stage and occasion |
| Messaging | By role and by buying stage (problem, solution, vendor, proof) | By segment and by moment (discover, choose, use, repeat) |
| Voice | Expert, clear, calm; no hype | Wider range; personality can lead |
| Visual | System that works in slides, documents, product UI, LinkedIn | System that works in packaging, social video, retail, app |
| Proof of success | Win rate, sales cycle, branded search, share of voice in category | Aided and unaided awareness, branded search, price premium, repeat rate |

## 5. Plays (repeatable work patterns)

1. **The onboarding brain** (Package A). Audit 30 to 50 existing assets. An agent extracts the voice and claims. A strategist cleans it up in 1 workshop. Load the brain. Calibrate the brand scorer on 20 good and 20 bad samples.
2. **The 3-route positioning sprint.** Research runs for 2 weeks. Agents draft 3 different routes, not 3 versions of 1 route. The client picks 1 in a 3-hour workshop.
3. **Market entry adaptation.** For a client who enters a new market (for example a UK brand into the GCC). Run a culture check, a language check and a trademark screen. Make the market versions of name, logo lockup and voice.
4. **Brand drift fix.** When the brand consistency score falls under target for 2 months, run a 1-week review. Find the cause (new team, new agency, weak templates). Fix templates and retrain the scorer.
5. **Merger or sub-brand architecture.** Decide on a branded house, a house of brands, or an endorsed model. Use the decision tree in `04-templates.md` §9.

## 6. What agents do and what people do

| Agents do | People do |
|---|---|
| Collect and summarise competitor, review and interview data | Run customer interviews and the workshop |
| Draft 3 positioning routes and messaging pillars | Choose the route; own the final words |
| Draft voice charts, vocabulary, taglines and name lists | Decide on the name and tagline |
| Screen names for bad meanings in 8 languages | Order the trademark search; the lawyer gives the opinion |
| Make mood boards and colour and type options | Design the logo and the final identity |
| Check contrast, sizes, file sets and template rules | Approve the identity and the guidelines |
| Compile the client brain and test it | Sign off the brain before other services use it |
| Score every asset for brand consistency, every day | Act on drift alerts |
